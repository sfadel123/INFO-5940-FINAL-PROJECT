When traveling, we have found that relying on online searches, travel articles, or social media recommendations often leads individuals to popular tourist destinations rather than experiences tailored to their specific interests. Many travelers seek immersive cultural experiences that allow them to engage with local communities and explore their personal interests. These experiences could range from a local art class to a specialized museum tour or a temporary pop-up event. However, discovering these opportunities often requires extensive research and knowledge of where to look. Our team aims to address this challenge by developing an LLM-powered travel planning application that provides personalized recommendations based on an individual’s interests, preferences, and travel plans.

Our target audience consists of individuals in their 20s and 30s who want to maximize their travel experiences without the expense of hiring a travel agent or the time commitment required to conduct extensive research. These travelers are interested in discovering unique, authentic experiences beyond the destinations commonly promoted through social media and traditional travel websites. Our proposed application will help bridge this gap by identifying relevant activities and experiences that align with each traveler’s interests, allowing users to create more personalized and meaningful travel experiences. An LLM is well suited to this problem because travelers describe their interests in fuzzy, personal terms, such as "quiet places to sketch," that do not map to the filters on standard travel sites. An LLM can turn these descriptions into targeted searches, read unstructured event listings and reviews, judge how well each option fits the traveler, and revise the plan through conversation.

Our application will integrate a Large Language Model to generate personalized travel recommendations for the user based on individual preferences. The LLM will first ask the user where they are traveling to, dates of travel, budget on activities, and interests. From there, we plan to have the LLM-powered agent connect to external tools, such as web-search, to gather information about local events and activities. The agent will then evaluate these activities based on the user’s interests and determine the best recommendation for the user. Our planned model is Qwen3 4B (qwen3:4b via Ollama), called with the OpenAI Python client through Ollama's OpenAI-compatible API. Its suitability, tool calling, and performance on our laptops will be tested during Construction; they have not yet been verified. The fallback is Qwen3 8B (qwen3:8b) on a laptop with more memory. This local connection requires no paid OpenAI API key and keeps our code in the standard OpenAI API format.

Together, the model and its search tool would enable the agent to interpret individual travel preferences, search for relevant events and activities, and generate personalized recommendations. Interests like "quiet places to sketch" or "I liked the pottery class in Lisbon" don't map to search filters. An LLM can turn that into targeted queries, read unstructured event listings and reviews, judge fit, and revise the plan through conversation. After receiving an itinerary, users can rate each activity with a thumbs up or thumbs down and an optional preset reason: "too touristy," "too expensive," "wrong time," or "not my interest." Free-text feedback is not collected. A thumbs-down marks an activity without immediately replacing it; clicking "Update itinerary" or asking in chat replaces only thumbs-down activities. A thumbs-up locks an activity, and unaffected activities and all preferences stay unchanged. Our team measures KPIs during evaluation rounds using 10 sample traveler profiles across 3 destinations, not with live users. The team selected New York City, London, and Paris, with ten sample profiles distributed 4/3/3. Activity-based KPIs use the final itinerary and latest ratings, with the full revision count recorded separately.

Our finalized product will be an LLM agent that provides users with a personalized and thorough travel itinerary based on their unique preferences. Our minimum viable product is three-fold: it will begin with a chatbot conversation, then the LLM will scour the internet for activities and pop-ups, and, finally, the LLM agent will build a customized itinerary. In this conversation, the chatbot will ask users about their travel destination, dates, budget, and interests, and ask follow-up questions when an answer is vague. Using these responses, an LLM-powered agent will leverage external web search tools to identify relevant activities and experiences, prioritizing activities that cater to the user interest over popular attractions. The application will then organize these into a formalized personalized daily itinerary. The traveler can select a budget currency; the app will preserve that input and compare the budget and activity prices in USD, labeling conversions as estimates with their rate source and date. The team selected the European Central Bank's documented daily reference-rate download, replacing the earlier planned WSJ source. A dated rate snapshot is cached for each trip and reused for revisions; if the entered budget cannot be converted, generation or revision pauses for correction or retry. The model proposes searches and activities within a Python-controlled flow, and the browser shows a waiting indicator while each generation request completes.
